THE CUTS

Know your cut. Cook it perfectly.

Premium Argentine Angus from the pampas. Each cut has its moment — here is how to bring out the best in every one.

◆

Picanha

Tapa de Cuadril · Cap of Rump

The crown of the Argentine asado. A tender, deeply flavorful cut with a signature fat cap that bastes the meat as it cooks.

Best method
Grill or roast, fat cap on
Doneness
Medium-rare, 130–135°F
Seasoning
Coarse salt, simple
Pampa tipScore the fat cap, sear it first to render, then finish over indirect heat. Rest 5–10 min and slice against the grain.

New York Strip

Bife Angosto · Striploin

Bold, beefy and beautifully marbled. The steakhouse classic — great char on the outside, juicy within.

Best method
High-heat grill or pan-sear
Doneness
Medium-rare, 130–135°F
Pairs with
Chimichurri
Pampa tipGet the grill screaming hot for a deep crust, flip once, and rest before cutting so the juices stay in.

Top Sirloin

Cuadril · Heart of Rump

Lean and full of flavor — an everyday hero that rewards a careful hand on the heat. Excellent for the whole table.

Best method
Grill or roast whole
Doneness
Medium-rare, ~130°F
Serve
Sliced thin, across grain
Pampa tipBecause it’s lean, don’t overcook it — pull it a touch early and let the carryover heat finish the job.

Tenderloin

Lomo · Filet

The most tender cut there is — buttery and elegant. The centerpiece for a Wellington, medallions, or a refined tartare.

Best method
Sear & finish, or roast
Doneness
Rare–medium-rare, 125–130°F
Shines as
Wellington · medallions
Pampa tipLean and quick-cooking — a hot sear then a gentle finish. Keep it rare to medium-rare to protect its delicate texture.

Temperatures are internal, measured with a thermometer, before resting. Meat is sold by weight — the final price reflects the actual pounds of your cut.
